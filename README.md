## What I build

I like the layer between a good idea and a dependable system: where models need evaluation, workflows need state, data needs structure and automation needs clear boundaries.

My work and experiments tend to converge around three areas:

| AI systems | Data platforms | Markets |
|---|---|---|
| Agents, orchestration, RAG, inference and evaluation | Python, Spark, Databricks, pipelines and production analytics | Market signals, catalysts, risk systems and post-trade learning |

Lately I have been going deeper on **GPU-accelerated inference, local agents, durable agentic workflows and the infrastructure required to make AI systems observable and trustworthy**.

## Building now

### [Brontide](https://github.com/Melvinroy/Journal)

**An open-source trading system built around the full decision loop — signal → risk → execution → learning.**

Brontide combines catalyst discovery, charting, position sizing, private journaling and post-trade analytics in a self-hostable workspace. The goal is not simply to record trades, but to make the reasoning around them measurable.

`Next.js` · `Supabase` · `Postgres` · `GitHub Actions` · `Market analytics`

### [Outreach Intelligence](https://github.com/Melvinroy/Outreach)

**A human-governed agentic workflow system for turning research signals into verified actions.**

It uses durable workflow state, LLM-assisted routing, explicit approval boundaries, event-based relationship history and supervised browser execution. The interesting problem is not generating a message; it is coordinating a multi-stage workflow without losing provenance, control or state.

`LangGraph` · `LLMs` · `Supabase` · `TypeScript` · `Browser automation`

## How I think about systems

- **Useful beats impressive.** A system should reduce ambiguity or make a decision better.
- **AI needs guardrails, not just prompts.** State, evaluation, validation and human control matter as much as the model.
- **Reliability is a feature.** I care about what happens after the demo works.
- **Build to learn.** The fastest way I understand a technology is usually by making something with it.
